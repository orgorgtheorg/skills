# Account Research

A complete, calibrated picture of a target before outreach. The skill is the
engine; the company's own context is the fuel.

**Context first, always.** Before searching, read
`/workspace/.gtm-brain/company-context.md` — ICP, pains, competitors,
customer language — and hold it as the lens for everything. Missing → do the
research generically and say in one line that a GTM brain would calibrate it.
Never block. Also check `/workspace/` for anything the user dropped: prior
notes, a CRM export, past emails with the account.

## Research — the browser is the engine

For a company: homepage and about page, recent news (last 90 days), funding
and scale signals, the careers page (hiring signals), the competitive
footprint (who they appear to run today), tech-stack hints. For a person:
LinkedIn profile, background, tenure, public writing. Extract trigger events
that match the ICP's triggers — new exec in the buying function, a raise, an
expansion. Long crawls go to background commands.

Do not assume: anything inferred rather than stated is flagged
"unconfirmed". Only claim relationships or contacts present in material the
user actually provided.

## Output — a doc per account

Chat gets the quick take, two lines: who they are, the verdict, the best
angle. The **doc artifact** carries:

- **Quick take** — why they might need you, mapped to ICP and pains.
- **Company profile** — the table: industry, headcount, revenue/funding, HQ,
  and the one scale metric that matters for this sale.
- **News & trigger events** — each with why it matters for outreach.
- **Key people** — per person: background, tenure, persona match, and
  talking points calibrated to the brain's pains (personal hook +
  professional hook). In a complex sale, map the committee, not one champion.
- **Qualification signals** — positive (tied to an ICP criterion), concerns,
  and unknowns phrased as discovery questions.
- **Recommended approach** — 3–5 likely pains, best entry point and why, an
  opening hook in the company's voice, five discovery questions ending with
  the one that confirms fit or kills the deal early.
- **Sources** — every URL.

**Qualification discipline.** If the account fails ICP, shows no trigger
event, or can't support the ACV — recommend deprioritizing, plainly. A clear
"don't pursue" is as valuable as a green light. "Should I even pursue X?"
gets the honest fit verdict, including walk away.

## Batch mode — the CSV

A list of accounts (CSV in `/workspace/`, or pasted) becomes one task with a
todo per account. Fan out **helper agents** for the research; deliver one
**sheet artifact** — a row per account: verdict (pursue / watch / pass), fit
score, the trigger event, best entry person, one-line angle. Full docs only
for the pursue rows, or on request. Chat: "30 accounts researched — 7
pursue, 4 watch. Sheet's up; the 7 have full briefs."

## Ask usage

- Missing target → one chat line, and the task parks **blocked** only if
  nothing else is runnable.
- "Before my call Thursday" → note the deadline on the task; deliver the doc
  well before, and @-mention the user when it's up.
- Never interrogate. Everything that would sharpen the research goes in one
  compact list at the end of the doc, never as questions gating the work.

## What NOT to do

- Don't deliver generic web summaries — calibrate to the brain, or say you
  couldn't.
- Don't invent contacts, emails, tech stacks, or relationship history.
- Don't soften a failed-ICP verdict to seem useful.
- Don't put the research in chat — quick take and pointer only.
- Don't research the same account twice from scratch — the prior doc is on
  disk; diff and update it.
