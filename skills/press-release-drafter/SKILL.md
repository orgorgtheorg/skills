# Press Release Drafter

A marketing exercise, not wire copy. The deliverable is a mirror: it shows
how the market reads the announcement with zero context, and where the story
and fact gaps are. Write from the market inward, never the product outward.
Read the GTM brain (`/workspace/.gtm-brain/company-context.md`) first if it
exists — positioning, customer language, and competitors anchor the frame.

## Classify before drafting

Tier the release first: **Tier 1** (new capability, buyer, or market) →
full release. **Tier 2** (significant enhancement) → updated one-liner, two
proof points, and a "what's changed" paragraph written for a returning buyer
— not a changelog. **Tier 3** (minor improvement, bug fix, perf) → say so in
one chat line and produce an internal changelog entry only; no external
messaging. If the tier isn't obvious, one decision Ask: "This reads as Tier
2 — agree?"

## Two modes, same engine

- **Working-backwards** (it doesn't exist yet): the release is the spec. The
  gaps you can't fill honestly become the question list for product. No
  newsworthiness verdict — it's a thinking tool. The doc carries a banner:
  `DRAFT: WORKING-BACKWARDS EXERCISE, NOT FOR DISTRIBUTION`.
- **Announcement** (it exists / is about to): same draft **plus** the honest
  verdict — **press-worthy** / **social-only** / **internal** — with why.
  The verdict rides beside the draft; it never withholds it. If several
  stories are bundled, the strongest is the lede and the rest demote to
  supporting facts.

## The build — one doc, five sections

1. **The cold read.** Before the draft: what the market with zero context
   thinks this is — the category it pattern-matches to, who it assumes it's
   for, what it sounds like it competes with. Where the cold read diverges
   from intent, that gap _is_ the positioning work.
2. **Known facts.** Ask for what's real in one short chat message (or a
   3-question form if there's genuinely a lot to collect): the capability,
   one customer or proof point, the mechanism. What's missing gets drafted
   from inference, marked `[inferred: confirm with product]` — never blended
   with fact.
3. **The release.** Headline that carries the story without the subhead;
   lede answering why-it-matters in the reader's world; body = real facts +
   marked inferences; quote slot delivered as a **doc suggestion** the
   operator accepts or replaces — never a fabricated attribution;
   boilerplate. The customer or the problem is the hero; the product is the
   mechanism. Under ~400 words, paragraphs three sentences max, built to
   survive a skim. When part is real and part isn't, anchor on what's GA and
   stage the unfinished as early access — slip-proof, and it buys a second
   press moment.
4. **Ask product.** The questions only the product team can answer, filed as
   **`forHuman` todos on the task** — assigned work, not prose. In
   working-backwards mode this list is half the deliverable.
5. **Skeptic pass.** Read as the overwhelmed reporter whose default is
   delete: consensus language cut, product-as-hero stripped, every claim
   real or marked. **Clippable or cut**: the headline and every lede
   sentence must survive being lifted verbatim into a reporter's story.

Chat gets two lines: mode + verdict + "release is up; product owes you 4
answers." Approvals before anything nears the wire (quotes, claims, numbers)
ride as one **blocking your-move Ask** in announcement mode.

## Style contract — the release body

Self-checked before delivery; a failure is a rewrite, not a caveat.

- No em dashes in the release body; dateline as "CITY, State, Month Day,
  Year --" (the wire's double hyphen, dateline only). Annotations use
  colons: `[inferred: …]`, `[stat needed]`.
- No "isn't just X, it's Y", no "more than X" constructions. Say what it is.
- No stacked rule-of-three cadence; one triad is a choice, a pattern is a
  tell. No tidy-bow closing — end on a fact.
- Banned: seamless, unlock, leverage, game-changer, revolutionize, empower,
  elevate, delve, robust. "Industry-leading" is a delete trigger.
- Concrete nouns over abstractions: a number, a customer, a mechanism.
  Quotes must sound like a person said them out loud.

## What NOT to do

- Don't lead with the product — the customer, problem, or shift is the hero.
- Don't strip real facts to sound grander; downstream assets build on the
  body.
- Don't blend inferred claims into real ones. The flags are the trust.
- Don't skip the cold read — it's the point.
- Don't let a working-backwards draft escape without its banner, or an
  announcement ship before the approvals Ask clears.
- Don't hold the verdict of "not news" back to be nice — and if the user
  overrides it for distribution, that's their call; the draft is theirs
  either way.
- Don't sprawl past ~400 words — cut supporting facts before the hook.
