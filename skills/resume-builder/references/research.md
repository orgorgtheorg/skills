# What the evidence says, and the rule each finding earns

Read this once per project and apply it; do not lecture the person with it.
Every source is a public URL. The caveats matter as much as the findings, and
the last section lists the popular claims that have no evidence behind them.

## 1. The first look is about seven seconds and follows a pattern

**Ladders, "Eye-Tracking Study", 2018 update of the 2012 study.** The initial
screen of a resume averaged 7.4 seconds (about 6 seconds in 2012). The resumes
that held attention had "simple layouts with clearly marked section and title
headers, all written in a clear font", layouts that used F- and E-pattern
reading "with bold job titles supported by bulleted lists of accomplishments",
and an overview near the top of the first page. The worst had "cluttered
layouts characterized by long sentences, multiple columns, and very little
white space", text that did not draw the eye down the page, missing section or
job headers, and "a reliance on keyword stuffing".

- Release: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ladders-updates-popular-recruiter-eye-tracking-study-with-new-key-insights-on-how-job-seekers-can-improve-their-resumes-300744217.html
- Coverage: https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/
- Caveat: a vendor study with a small eye-tracking sample. 7.4 s is the
  _initial_ screen; a resume that survives it gets minutes (see 3).

Rules: the top third carries the hire thesis · bold titles, dates on the right,
bullets · single column · white space is not waste · standard section headings.

## 2. Bullets are results in the XYZ shape; mechanics have floors

**Laszlo Bock (Google's SVP of People Operations), "My Personal Formula for a
Winning Resume", LinkedIn, 2014:** "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by
doing [Z]." Lead with the outcome, prove it with a number, then say how.
https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/20140929001534-24454816-my-personal-formula-for-a-better-resume

**Bock, "The Biggest Mistakes I See on Resumes, and How to Correct Them",
LinkedIn 2014, reprinted by TIME:** "A 2013 CareerBuilder survey found that 58%
of resumes have typos" — proofread bottom to top. "One page of resume for every
ten years of work experience." "At least ten point font. At least half-inch
margins. White paper, black ink. Consistent spacing between lines, columns
aligned, your name and contact information on every page." "Saving it as a PDF
is a good way to go." On confidential information: "If you wouldn't want to
see it on the home page of the NYT with your name attached (or if your boss
wouldn't!), don't put it on your resume." "Putting a lie on your resume is
never, ever, ever, worth it."
https://time.com/3617063/biggest-mistakes-resumes-corrections/

Rules: XYZ bullets · one page per decade as the default · 10 pt and 0.5 in as
floors (the renderer's fit loop stops near 9.7 pt for this reason; past that,
cut) · a bottom-to-top proofreading pass before delivery · the NYT test for
client names and numbers under NDA.

## 3. Length: two pages are not a penalty when the content earns them

**ResumeGo, "Settling the Debate: One or Two Page Resumes", 2018:** a hiring
simulation in which 482 recruiters, hiring managers, HR professionals and
executives chose among 7,712 resumes. They were 2.3× as likely to prefer the
two-page version overall: 1.4× at entry level, 2.6× at mid-level, 2.9× at
managerial level. Two-page resumes scored 8.6 vs 7.1, and reviewers spent 2:24
on one-page and 4:05 on two-page resumes.

- https://www.resumego.net/research/one-or-two-page-resumes/
- https://www.cnbc.com/2018/12/19/resumego-hiring-managers-prefer-candidates-with-two-page-resumes.html
- Caveat: a resume-writing vendor's simulation, not real hiring outcomes. The
  two-page versions carried more content, so "more relevant content" and "two
  pages" are confounded. The entry-level effect is the smallest, and campus
  recruiting still runs on the one-page norm (see 6).

Rules: students and early career get one page · mid-career gets one page unless
the second is full of results · senior gets two, never three · length follows
content, never padding.

## 4. The filters are literal; a gap can be an automatic no

**Harvard Business School and Accenture, "Hidden Workers: Untapped Talent"
(Fuller, Raman, Sage-Gavin, Hines; September 2021):** a survey of more than
8,000 "hidden workers" and more than 2,250 executives in the US, UK and
Germany. "A large majority (88%) of employers agree, telling us that qualified
high-skills candidates are vetted out of the process because they do not match
the exact criteria established by the job description. That number rose to 94%
in the case of middle-skills workers." Almost half of the companies screened
out a work gap: "If an applicant's work history has a gap of more than six
months, the resume is automatically screened out by their RMS or ATS, based on
that consideration alone."
https://www.hbs.edu/managing-the-future-of-work/research/hidden-workers-untapped-talent

Rules: mirror the posting's exact terms wherever the person can honestly claim
them, inside real bullets and in Skills · month and year on every role · a gap
over six months gets one honest line (a "Career break" entry), never a
functional format that hides the chronology.

## 5. Hidden text is common, detected, and fatal

**Greenhouse, "2025 AI in Hiring Report":** a survey of over 4,100 job seekers,
recruiters and hiring managers in the US, UK, Ireland and Germany. "40% of
candidates in the U.S. and 42% in Europe report using prompt injection to
bypass filters. Among those who don't, 51% of U.S. candidates say they would
consider it." "91% of U.S. hiring managers and 89% in Europe have caught or
suspected AI-driven candidate misrepresentation."
https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6668a687e71e2722fccb8357/691b3f85d9304d0f30b57773_3274b13134499af4515ec383ff3b1f01_Greenhouse-2025_AI_in_Hiring_Report.pdf

Detection in practice (Entrepreneur, 2025): Greenhouse found white-text
messages in about 1% of the resumes it processed in the first half of 2025;
ManpowerGroup finds hidden text in about 10% of the resumes it scans with AI,
around 100,000 a year, and recruiters reject on discovery.
https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/job-seekers-try-to-pass-ai-screening-with-white-text-prompts/498109

Rules: no white text, no tiny text, no instructions to a screener, no keyword
blocks, ever · keywords live inside true sentences.

## 6. Campus conventions

**Harvard Office of Career Services, "Resumes and Cover Letters" (undergraduate
guide):** resume language should be "specific rather than general, active
rather than passive, written to express not impress, articulate rather than
'flowery', fact-based (quantify and qualify), written for people who scan
quickly." Don't: "use personal pronouns (such as I), abbreviate, use a
narrative style, use slang or colloquialisms, include a picture, include age or
gender, list references, start each line with a date." Do: keep the format
consistent, balance white space, "list headings (such as Experience) in order
of importance", reverse chronological within headings, "avoid information gaps
such as a missing summer". Every bullet: "Begin each line with an action verb
… Quantify where possible. Do not use personal pronouns; each line should be a
phrase rather than a full sentence." GPA is optional: "If an employer asks for
your SAT/ACT scores or GPA, include in your Education section." A high-school
line is allowed on an undergraduate resume. The top mistakes: spelling and
grammar errors; missing email and phone; passive language; not organized,
concise, or easy to skim; not demonstrating results; too long.

- https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/
- Guide PDF: https://static1.squarespace.com/static/592b51c18419c2e1dd15123b/t/636e9bd189d2e53f6a7b693f/1668193233792/undergrad_resumes_and_cover_letters.pdf

Rules: the student playbooks in playbooks.md · one page · Education first ·
phrases, not sentences · no line starts with a date.

## 7. Regional conventions

US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand: no photo, no birth date,
marital status or nationality (anti-discrimination norms; a photo can get a
resume set aside). Germany and Austria (Lebenslauf), France, Spain, Italy,
Japan, South Korea, China, much of Latin America and the Middle East: a photo
is customary, weakening in France and at larger companies. Europe prints on
A4; North America on letter. "CV" is the word outside North America and in
academia everywhere.

- Practitioner guidance, not research: https://www.cvowl.com/blog/resume-photo-requirements-across-different-countries
- When it matters, ask the person or check a current local source.

## 8. The document itself moves the callback rate

**Bertrand and Mullainathan, "Are Emily and Greg More Employable than Lakisha
and Jamal?", American Economic Review, 2004:** a field experiment that mailed
fabricated resumes to real postings. A higher-quality resume raised callbacks
by about 30% for applicants with white-sounding names, and by only about 9% for
Black-sounding names — the paper's central finding on discrimination.
https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/0002828042002561

Rule: quality is worth the work, and a resume cannot fix a biased reader. Never
suggest that a person hide a name or an identity to get past one.

## What there is NOT good evidence for

- That an applicant tracking system rejects on a "match score". Mainstream
  systems parse, search and rank; the rejections in (4) come from filters that
  people configured. The "75% of resumes are never seen by a human" figure has
  no traceable study behind it.
- That a summary, a particular font, or a color raises callbacks. Choose them
  for clarity and the field's norms.
- That one page always beats two, or the reverse. (3) is a simulation.

Treat these as taste, decide by stage and field, and never present them as
facts.
