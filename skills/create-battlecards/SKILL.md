# Battle Cards

If it doesn't fit on a page a rep can scan before a call, it's a research
memo, not a battle card. Synthesis, not summary — every line tells the rep
what to **say** or **do**. Truth is the floor: claims sourced and dated, or
flagged.

**Inputs, in order of preference:** the living intel brief at
`/workspace/.gtm-brain/intel/<competitor>/brief.md` (verified rows and dates
carry straight in — check for it yourself, don't ask); the GTM brain for what
we sell and our differentiators; fresh browser research where both are thin;
any call snippets or win/loss notes in `/workspace/`. Two company names is
enough to start — research the rest.

## The run

File a task (moves / fight / objections / landmines / verify) and build the
card in one pass as a **doc artifact** at
`/workspace/.gtm-brain/battlecards/<competitor>.md`. Don't pause for
approval between sections — the user steers by replying mid-run if they want
to. Run the skeptic pass as you go: each claim **Ownable** (not something
five competitors could say), **Sourced** (dated, right tier), **Distinctive**
(reject "more secure" consensus framing).

**Defend your own landmines too.** The card isn't complete until it preps the
attack the _competitor_ will make on _you_ — the public breach, the pricing
sore spot, the known gap. One honest "when they raise <our landmine> → the
truthful answer" row saves more deals than a sixth fight row.

## The card (one page — hard limit)

```
BATTLE CARD: <Competitor> · <date> · Verdict: win / parity / watch

WIN THE DEAL — the 3 moves    (lead here in any live call)
THE FIGHT     5–6 rows: dimension — our edge + why · their reality ·
              proof+date · the rep move/landmine. Include where you're
              parity or behind; honesty is the point.
IF THEY SAY → YOU SAY   top 3 objections
              DON'T FIGHT HERE: <where you lose> → redirect to <your wedge>
VERIFY BEFORE YOU WALK IN   2–3 load-bearing claims, each with its source
```

Body stays within one page and roughly **350 words**. The shape above is the
ceiling, not the floor — no sections beyond it unless asked. When in doubt,
cut. Zero meta-commentary, zero process notes, zero apologies in the
rep-facing card.

Chat gets one line: "Card's up — verdict: <win/parity/watch>, lead with move
1." The VERIFY list also goes out as a **non-blocking your-move Ask** so it
counts as something waiting on a human, not a paragraph they scroll past.

**Customer-facing export (on request):** fight rows only, verified claims
only, honest parity — never a faked win. Before it leaves the building, raise
a **blocking your-move Ask**: "needs PMM/legal review — competitor mentions
in customer-facing material."

## Segment cut (on request)

"Card vs X for EMEA mid-market" gets a **complete finished cut** as its own
one-page doc — every row re-fought through the segment lens (compliance,
data residency, local presence, size-appropriate pricing framing), rows the
segment genuinely doesn't move left as-is. Same page/word ceiling. Where a
segment claim is inferred from general evidence, tag it `[unverified]`
inline and add one short "do not say" line for claims the evidence can't
support. If the evidence is too thin to cut honestly, say the base card is
the better card and why — a cut isn't owed just because one was asked for.

## Sourcing

Perception claims → prospect/customer voice beats the web. Hard facts →
primary source wins; a single claim is a lead to verify. Recency wins;
negative claims carry the highest bar — they may have shipped it last month.
Unverified ships visibly flagged, never faked.

## What NOT to do

- Don't exceed a page, ~350 words, or ~6 fight rows.
- Don't fake a win — honest parity beats an edge the prospect can disprove.
- Don't fill 6 fight rows from one signal — an honest 3-row fight beats 6
  padded ones.
- Don't invent a delta on "refresh" — only diff against the card on disk.
- Don't bury the 3 moves; they lead, everything else is backup.
- Don't redesign the card into a memo, add sections, or let working notes
  ship inside it.
- Don't ship a half-cut segment card — complete, or recommend the base card.
